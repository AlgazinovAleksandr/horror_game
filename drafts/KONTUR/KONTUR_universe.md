# K.O.N.T.U.R. (Контур) Universe – Analytical Report

**Executive Summary:** CONTUR is a Russian-language analog-horror universe created by artist Gleb Khasanov, framed as the archives of a secret Soviet-era organization.  Its core premise centers on a fungus called O-41 that infects concrete, warping familiar spaces (apartments, stairwells, vehicles) into endless, illogical dimensions populated by horror creatures.  The story is told through found-footage–style videos and “instructional” recordings (on Telegram, YouTube, etc.), set in recognizable Soviet/Post-Soviet environments like Khrushchyovka apartment blocks and dark elevators.  The universe’s acronym, **K.O.N.T.U.R.**, stands for *Kontrol’ Obʺektov Neizvestnogo Tipa i Uslovnoj Real’nosti* (“Control of Objects of Unknown Type and Conditional Reality”).  This report covers CONTUR’s origins and media history, its cosmic-horror lore (the O-41 fungus and its anomalous “objects”), major organizations (the KONTUR agency and related bodies), key entities/“monsters,” a chronological timeline of in-universe events, the setting’s technologies and rules, thematic influences (Soviet nostalgia meets Lovecraftian horror), narrative structure, fan reception, and open questions. All claims are cited from official and community sources. 

## Origin and Publication History  
CONTUR originated in the mid-2020s as a multimedia horror project.  Its creator is Russian artist **Gleb Khasanov**, who began releasing short horror clips and “archival” videos via social platforms (notably the @lost_archive_ru YouTube channel and the Telegram channel Проект К.О.Н.Т.У.Р.).  These videos mix vertical-format “story clips” with longer found-footage recordings styled as internal memos, training films, or radio logs from the secret agency.  According to Vedomosti magazine (July 2026), the universe’s story *was not fully pre-written* from the start: early clips were made as standalone experiments, and only after the 4th–6th video did the team “start fleshing out a consistent world and plot”.  By around the sixth episode, the creators retroactively wove earlier footage into a single continuity and established a lore framework.  

The setting is explicitly placed in a USSR/post-Soviet timeline: a special Soviet government committee founded the K.O.N.T.U.R. organization in the mid-1970s to contain anomalous phenomena.  (While detailed sources are scarce, the project lore states it was launched by a 1976 Council-of-Ministers order.)  Over the decades KONTUR moved from a strictly Soviet structure into a post-Soviet agency.  The narrative videos thus span from roughly the late Cold War through the present.  In-universe, the team describes KONTUR’s archives and materials as “realistic” and “historically plausible”. In parallel, the project’s real-world publication is taking place in 2022–2026: as of mid-2026 there are over 70,000 Telegram subscribers and hundreds of thousands of Instagram/YouTube followers.  A major June–July 2026 Vedomosti feature introduced CONTUR to a wider audience. 

## Core Lore and Cosmology  

 *Figure: A typical Soviet-era apartment block (mid-20th century Khrushchyovka). CONTUR’s horror occurs in such ordinary post-Soviet settings – from dark stairwells to run-down clinics and factories.*  

At the heart of the CONTUR universe is **Spore Fungus O-41**, a microbial agent that invades concrete and other building materials. Infected areas become *“Objects”* – self-contained anomalous spaces where normal physics break down.  An apartment, bus, or office becomes an endless, looped corridor or maze, reconstructed by the fungus “as it understands it,” so everything looks familiar yet obeys warped rules.  Within these spaces dwell bizarre creatures (detailed below) that serve the fungus’s implicit “agenda.”  In effect, each Object is a pocket-dimension with a layered structure: a **surface level** (what infected people first see), a deeper “technical” level where the simulation is constructed, and even a mythical subterranean **mycelial core** anchoring the network.  

KONTUR personnel study and try to contain these Objects.  They have documented that any mirrored surface or other triggers can cause disorientation in the infected.  Conventional weaponry *inside* an object tends to fail (the space simply “recreates” or spawns the monster), so agents rely on surveillance, traps and evacuation protocols.  Household acids (notably vinegar) are shown to **slow** the spore’s spread (though not cure it).  Overall, the lore frames the fungus as a near-sentient ecosystem: it “simulates” environments, creates phantom inhabitants, and mutates people or animals exposed to it into new entities.  

The major **Entities/Monsters** created by O-41 are named by KONTUR in mundane Russian terms. Each has a specific “function” in the infection ecology:

- **Zarazhënnıy-0** *(“Infected-0” or simply “Zero”)* – A gaunt, elongated humanoid first tracked in the late 1990s on Siberian roads.  It lurks in isolated areas, preys on lone travelers at night, and causes people to vanish. In 2004 it was photographically identified, triggering a KONTUR “Operation Carpet” to chase it (with casualties). Afterward, KONTUR established a containment protocol: when Zero appears, they cordon off the area, blaming it on roadworks.  
- **Kanalotvar** *(“Sewage Creature”)* – Born in the late 1980s when spore-contaminated water touched a stray animal. This monster inhabits narrow dark spaces (especially rusted pipe tunnels), lying in wait for victims over long periods. In 2012 it was sighted in Novosibirsk, prompting a city-wide campaign to seal off all trash chutes and sewer entry points.  
- **Perëkozhnik** *(“Shapechanger”)* – A creature that perfectly mimics a human or domestic figure but then stands unnaturally still (for example in a corner). When someone approaches or looks away, it suddenly attacks to drag the victim into the Object. It plays on the uncanny valley (it seems normal at first glance but feels wrong).  
- **Gabar** – Resembling a toy-like cartoon character, Gabar has a rounded childlike face (like a plush toy) with long dangling tentacles. It captures victims by grabbing them if they stop moving; even a momentary pause draws its attention. It seems linked to childlike or “happy” emotions twisted by the fungus.  
- **Falʹsh** *(“Falseness”)* – A large, ghostly humanoid with an enormous mouth. Its face is expressionless or ambiguously smiling/crying, making it terrifyingly inscrutable. It is thought to embody mixed or suppressed emotions in an office or communal setting.  
- **Vyazʹ** *(“Bind”)* – A giant, spider-like monstrosity of Lovecraftian scale. With many long limbs, Vyaz represents cosmic dread; it appears in heavily infected Objects when the fungus grows unchecked.  

(Each creature’s Russian name was chosen to sound like a bureaucratic code.  The wiki notes the names are mundane or even affectionate to mimic how agency records might label them.)  

Other anomalies include **Simulacra** (perfect “ghosts” of people, as in early Object experiments with a girl named Masha) and **Mimikry** (entities that latch onto victims’ emotions). Overall, every monster is presented as part of the fungus system – none are random. (The Vedomosti interview emphasizes that even monster designs come from these imagined “functions” rather than straight Cthulhu lifts.)

## Anomalous Systems and Technology  

 *Figure: A concrete wall infested with mold and moss – analogous to how O-41 fungal infection visibly appears on buildings. The lore suggests such growth is the fungus spreading through porous concrete.*  

The primary “technology” of the CONTUR universe is **biological/anomalous** rather than mechanical. The O-41 fungus itself is the generative mechanism: it remodels reality from the inside out.  Agents use standard equipment (cameras, seismic sensors, etc.) to study Objects, but many conventional tools fail in an anomaly.  For example, weapons have near-zero effect: “the space *can recreate* the monster,” so shooting just delays the outcome. Instead, KONTUR often relies on subterfuge (e.g. concealing cameras as bug exterminators) and containment protocols.  

Medical science in-continuity recognizes **Spore Syndrome**: humans absorbing too many O-41 spores suffer neurological disorientation and dementia.  A notable quirk is that common kitchen vinegar (acetic acid) is shown to **slow down** the mold’s spread on surfaces.  (In reality vinegar’s antifungal effect is mild; the story uses it as a plausible home remedy.)  This means ordinary people trapped in an infected building can buy time by spraying vinegar around thresholds.  The writers explicitly say vinegar *does not cure* the infection, merely delays it.  

Within an Object, spatial physics are governed by the fungus’s perception.  The setting is effectively a stratified virtual space: a **near layer** matching familiar geometry, and a **deep layer** that reassembles structure.  At an even deeper level lies the fungus’s **mycelial network** – a quasi-mythical core said to connect all infected areas.  This deep layer might explain how corridors loop infinitely.  In short, the “magic system” is the fungus’s geometry: it unconsciously *simulates* an environment (like a video game world) as a manifestation of its organic logic.  

No supernatural forces beyond O-41 are described; the horror is rooted in distorted science.  (Although not explicitly stated, the style nods to the SCP Foundation mythos: an organization cataloguing anomalous objects with cold precision.)  All anomaly behavior is ultimately attributed to the spore mechanics.  Any ordinary technology (radios, explosives, surveillance) functions normally *up to* the infection’s boundary but is overwhelmed inside.  For example, even a fully-armed squad entering a medium-level Object was noted to fail in containing a critter, so KONTUR personnel emphasize stealth and intelligence gathering instead.  

## Themes, Influences, and Genre  

CONTUR deliberately mines **post-Soviet cultural imagery** for horror.  As creator Gleb Khasanov notes, many Russians recognize the dread of a **dark apartment entryway**, a broken street lamp, or a smelly trash chute from childhood fears.  By placing supernatural events in *ordinary spaces* (old Khrushchev buildings, soviet-era factories, rural outhouses), the project resonates as a “hidden subgenre” of horror that reflects the local milieu.  This Russian-centrism (e.g. communal apartments, Soviet military outfits, Cyrillic signage) is cited as a factor in the project’s popularity: it’s horror in a familiar language and setting.  

Genre-wise, CONTUR is **analog horror**: it uses the aesthetic of found video and archival media.  The style recalls internet creepypasta, faux-documentary VHS tapes, and the SCP Foundation’s secret files.  Fans and some analysts explicitly call it a *“Russian SCP”* analogy.  Its format (short inexplicable clips, followed by explanatory logs) mirrors Western series like *Local58* or *Marble Hornets*, but with Soviet textures.  Thematically it blends **cosmic horror** (monster gigantism, unnameable fungus) with **bureaucratic thriller** (forms, instructions, cover-ups).  There is a clear Lovecraftian influence in the creature scale – for example, the monster *Vyazʹ* reflects “Lovecraftian gigantism”.  However, monsters are not random Lovecraft entities but are grounded in twisted human concepts (“office smiles,” childhood toys).  

Because KONTUR is framed as a secret archive, *linguistic style* is also a theme: many terms are acronyms or codes (e.g. O-41, ZKI: Zakharovo Isolated).  The universe stresses the horror of conformity and isolation under authority.  Unlike many analog-horror series with a lone protagonist, CONTUR’s narrative focus is the **organization itself**.  This makes the suspense about *institutional culpability* – e.g. agents covering up disasters, or government vetoing evacuations for “strategic” reasons.  Thus common horror tropes (haunted house, monster chase) are infused with Cold War-style paranoia and black-ops experimentation.  

## Narrative Structure and Notable Works  

CONTUR’s story is conveyed **episodically** through videos, rather than in a linear novel.  The releases fall into two main types: **short horror clips** (YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, etc.) that demonstrate a single anomaly or shock image, and **longer “archival” videos** (10–15 minutes) that pose as internal memos, training films, or news clips.  For instance, one video is a mock “civil defense” lesson for workers dealing with mid-level infection (complete with Soviet-era instructional narration).  Another is an internal safety briefing on how to handle mirror-induced hysteria in early-stage cases.  None of these videos focus on named human protagonists; they are framed as institutional artifacts.  

According to interviews, the creators plan a multi-phase narrative.  Initially they are establishing “the world rules” for the audience.  Only later will they introduce recurring human characters and a continuous plotline.  The lead developer states the project is structured in **three acts**: 1) introduction to the world (done via the current shorts and docs); 2) a story with recurring characters (future longer episodes or a vertical series); 3) a conclusion (planned endpoint already known to the team).  As of 2026, permanent characters are not yet introduced, although “cameo” scientists and officers appear unnamed in some videos.  

In the real world, CONTUR’s notable “works” to date are these videos and social-media posts.  The Instagram and Telegram channels often link each short to a related text blurb or thread.  An official Fandom wiki (Russian) catalogs the lore.  There are no published books, movies, or games **yet**, but the creators report that they are pursuing licensing deals for games, films and novels based on the CONTUR universe.  The project is self-financed through crowdfunding (Boosty platform) and occasional ads, with profits earmarked for expanding the brand.  (As noted, they decline any sponsor that would break the immersion, such as casinos.)  

## Reception and Fan Community  

CONTUR has rapidly built a dedicated fanbase.  By mid-2026, it boasted hundreds of thousands of followers across Telegram, Instagram, VK, and YouTube.  Fans create detailed analyses, fan-art compilations (as shown in the official Telegram channel), and translations.  A Vedomosti feature (“Monster from the Garbage Chute,” July 2026) signaled mainstream interest.  Russian-language fan forums compare CONTUR to the SCP Foundation and analog-horror series, calling it an ambitious post-Soviet horror mythology.  English-language awareness is limited but growing via horror enthusiast channels (some videos have unofficial subtitles).  

The official team actively engages fans: they regularly repost fan art (as in their community update posts) and answer FAQs (e.g. confirming the acronym’s meaning and stance on AI-generated content).  A dedicated CONTUR wiki (контурproject.fandom.com) has dozens of articles in Russian summarizing lore and objects.  Online discussion threads (e.g. on Reddit’s Russian forums) show fans eagerly speculating on KONTUR’s secrets and hoping for a video game adaptation.  There is, however, little sign of any official Western coverage beyond fan interest.  Overall reception is very positive among Russian horror fans, who praise its originality and atmosphere.  

## Open Questions and Contradictions  

Because CONTUR’s narrative is still unfolding, many details remain **uncertain**.  The ultimate origin of the O-41 fungus (why it appeared in Soviet times) is not explicitly stated.  The supposed closed-city experiments (e.g. Ark-1) are only partially revealed (the wiki hints at children and families moved into infected blocks in the 1990s, but official videos only tease it).  The author acknowledges that “not all the invented material has been shown in videos yet” – many story pieces await release.  

Another open question is the scale of the mythic **mycelium network**.  While it is referenced as the “heart” of the infection, its nature and location are only described abstractly.  It’s unclear if there is one central root (perhaps under Russia) or a distributed system.  The series also has yet to show long-term solutions or fail-states (for example, a never-before-asked question: what if an Object becomes fully infinite?).  

No direct contradictions have been noted among the official materials (the creators are careful to maintain consistency).  However, fans do point out minor timeline gaps (e.g. there are no reported incidents in the early 1970s leading up to KONTUR’s founding).  Similarly, the role of certain terms (like “Spore Syndrome” vs. “КОЛ”) could be clearer.  At least one Reddit commenter noted confusion over CONTUR’s acronym-style name (calling it a trendy convention) – the creators replied that it’s simply a naming choice, not a copy of others.  

In summary, CONTUR’s current canon covers the basics but leaves much to imagine.  Important questions – how the Soviet agency handled earlier outbreaks, what happened after 2000s crises, and who (if anyone) will emerge as protagonists – are intentionally held in reserve.  Future releases are expected to address these as the project moves into character-driven storytelling.
